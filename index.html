What every skill shares

Designed to sharpen your judgment

These skills follow a common set of Stanford Law principles. AI is a tool in the toolbox — useful for some clearly defined tasks, inappropriate for others, and always subject to your judgment and accountability.

The PAUSE Rule

Pause before reaching for a tool: define the problem and weigh non-AI alternatives first.

Policy-aware

Grounded in the SLS student AI policy and Stanford's Responsible AI guidance.

Verification-first

Every factual and legal claim gets checked in authoritative sources — model agreement is not proof.

Productive struggle

You attempt the work first. The skills scaffold learning; they never do your assessed work.

Transparent logs

Each session produces a learning-evidence record you can keep and disclose.

Accurate, not anthropomorphic

Plain-language explanations of how AI works — tokens, weights, and pattern-based generation.

Getting started

From download to first session

Every package is a validated skill.zip containing its SKILL.md, ChatGPT interface metadata, and supporting Stanford Law references.

1

Download the skill

Click the button on any card to download that skill's skill.zip straight from this repository.

2

Import into your assistant

Load the skill into your AI assistant — for example as a ChatGPT project or a Claude Skill — using the bundled metadata.

3

Start from your problem

Open with the task you're actually trying to learn. The skill applies the PAUSE Rule and policy checks before anything else.

4

Keep your evidence

Save the learning-evidence record the skill produces so your AI use stays transparent and disclosable.

Access to RCLL-provided tools is limited to current SLS community members and ends after graduation; requests are manually approved and authenticate with Stanford SSO / SUNet. Do not assume every publicly advertised vendor feature is enabled for SLS, and always confirm your course's AI policy with the instructor. For AI tools, access, technical help, tool selection, or legal-research support, contact the Robert Crown Law Library.
